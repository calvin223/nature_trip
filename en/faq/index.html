About the trips

Where do your trips go?
Our home ground is the Western Ghats of South India, across Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, where our naturalists have done much of their field research. Alongside the Western Ghats Endemics Expedition we run a monsoon rainforest trip for frogs and reptiles, a Central India big-cat safari and a Himalayan birding trip. Custom routes can be discussed on request.
Will we definitely see tigers, or the species listed on a trip page?
Wildlife sightings depend on the season, the weather and local conditions on the day of your visit. We plan routes and timings to give you the best chance, but no particular sighting can be guaranteed. The species on each trip page are what the habitats hold and what we will look for, not a promise. Whatever turns up on the day, your naturalist will help you find it, identify it and understand how it lives.
When is the best time to go?
It depends on the trip. Western Ghats Endemics runs from October to January; we stop in February and March because Eravikulam National Park closes each year for the Nilgiri tahr calving season. Monsoon Rainforest Nights runs from June to September, when frogs and reptiles are most active and the forest is at its greenest. Central India Big Cats runs from October to May: tiger-reserve core zones close for the monsoon from 1 July to 30 September, and late-May days can pass 40 °C. The Himalaya trip runs from March to May and in October and November.
How academic are the tours?
They are experience-led, not lecture-led. Guides explain what you are seeing in the field, but the emphasis is on wildlife watching, photography, landscapes, food, rest and comfortable travel.
Apart from wildlife, I want to see beautiful places. Is that included?
Yes. Scenic places are part of each route, not an extra. On the Western Ghats Endemics Expedition you pass through the shola forests and grasslands of Pampadum Shola and Mukurthi, Rajamalai in Eravikulam National Park, the tea-estate country around Valparai, and Doddabetta and Wenlock Downs near Ooty. The monsoon trip includes a waterfall or viewpoint visit and stops in spice and plantation country; the Himalaya trip follows a ridge route with mountain views. Each trip page shows the route day by day in its itinerary. If landscapes matter more to you than wildlife, we can weight a private itinerary towards them.
Can you provide guided day tours?
Yes. Where local logistics allow, we offer half-day and full-day guiding for birds, butterflies, forest trails, wetlands and safaris, for guests who are already in the area.
Can you design a programme for our group?
Yes. We design custom programmes for families, private groups, photographers, clubs, companies, schools and universities. Tell us your dates, group size, comfort level and interests.

Who can join

I have a six-month-old baby. Can we join, and what is the minimum age?
Our fixed departures are not suitable for babies and toddlers. Days involve long drives on winding hill roads, pre-dawn starts, open safari vehicles and stays in remote areas that can be hours from a hospital with children's care. Our minimum age on fixed departures is 8, and 12 for night walks, which means the Monsoon Rainforest Nights trip is for ages 12 and over. Everyone under 18 travels with a parent or guardian. Some forest-department treks set their own higher limits (Tamil Nadu forest treks, for example, generally exclude children under 10), so a few walks may not be open to younger children. If you are travelling with a baby, ask us about a private, gentle itinerary: short drives, one comfortable base and daytime walks, with nothing that depends on early starts. Please also talk to your doctor about travel and vaccinations for your child.
I am 75 and walk slowly. Can I join?
Age on its own is not a barrier; pace and health are what matter. The Central India Big Cats trip is rated Easy: most wildlife watching is from a safari vehicle, and walks are short and optional. Our Moderate trips include walks on uneven forest trails, some climbs and cool early starts at altitude. The guide sets a pace the whole group can manage, and you can sit out any walk. If you would rather set your own pace throughout, a private departure is the better fit. Getting into an open safari jeep involves a high step. Please tell us about any mobility limits or medical conditions before booking, and check that your travel insurance covers you at your age, as some policies have upper age limits.
We are a family and want to see wildlife in the forest without much risk. Is there a suitable trip?
Yes. The Central India Big Cats & Forest Safari is our most family-friendly route: it is rated Easy, wildlife is watched from safari vehicles with a naturalist, walks stay in lodge grounds and buffer forest, and there is downtime between drives. Our guides keep a safe distance from animals, follow park rules and never handle wildlife. For younger children or a gentler pace, a private family itinerary or a few guided day walks may suit you better. The minimum age on fixed departures is 8.
Do I need previous wildlife knowledge?
No. Most trips are beginner-friendly, and our guides help you see, identify and enjoy wildlife at your level. Experienced naturalists can still ask for specialist routes and deeper interpretation.
What level of fitness do I need?
It depends on the trip. The Central India safari is rated Easy and is mostly vehicle-based. The rainforest, Western Ghats and Himalaya trips are Moderate, with more walking on uneven ground, some climbs and more exposure to weather. Each trip page shows its difficulty. On a private departure we can tailor the pace.
Do you welcome international visitors?
Yes. Our trips are planned with overseas travellers in mind and are guided in English. For the Western Ghats Endemics Expedition, the nearest airport to the start at Thattekad is Kochi (COK); the trip ends at Bandipur, about 80 km from Mysuru, with onward travel via Mysuru or Bengaluru (BLR). For the other trips we confirm the start point and nearest gateway when you book. You are responsible for your own visa: apply only through the official site, indianvisaonline.gov.in. Carry your passport on park days, as entry permits for foreign nationals are checked against it.

Food & diet

I am vegan. Will I get food I can eat?
Yes, with notice. Indian cooking has a strong vegetarian tradition, and many everyday dishes can be made without dairy: idli, dosa, sambar and coconut-based vegetable curries in the south; dal, chana and vegetable dishes with roti elsewhere. Ghee, butter, paneer, curd and milk in tea are common, though, so tell us at booking and we will brief every lodge and kitchen on the route. Menus at remote lodges are simpler than in towns, and we cannot promise a separate vegan kitchen, but we will plan so that you have a proper meal at every stop.
I cannot eat spicy food. Will there be something I can enjoy?
Yes. Tell us when you book and we will ask each kitchen to make mild versions of dishes for you. Plain rice, chapati, dal, vegetables, curd, eggs and toast are easy to find in most lodges, and your guide can point out which dishes on a shared table are mild. If you are unsure about a dish, just ask before you try it.
Are meals included?
Yes. Meals are included on all four trips (each trip page lists exactly what is included). Food is usually fresh, local and vegetarian-friendly. Tell us about any allergy, intolerance or dietary need when you book.

Accessibility

Do you have tours suitable for disabled travellers?
We do not yet run trips designed for wheelchair users, and we would rather say so plainly than promise access we have not checked. Forest trails are uneven, open safari jeeps have a high step, and many lodges and park facilities have steps and bathrooms that are not adapted. Some parts of our trips involve little walking: jeep safaris once you are aboard, wildlife watching from the vehicle, birding in lodge gardens, and interpretation centres such as the Salim Ali Bird Interpretation Centre at Thattekad and those at Eravikulam and Bandipur. We have not yet assessed these for step-free access. Please tell us about your needs. We will be honest about what is realistic and, where it is, build a private itinerary around it.

Health & safety

Do I need travel insurance?
Yes, it is essential. Every guest must hold comprehensive travel medical insurance for the whole trip, covering medical treatment, hospital stays and emergency evacuation from remote areas. Check that it covers the activities on your trip and, where relevant, your age and any pre-existing conditions.
I have allergies. What should I tell you?
Tell us at booking about any allergy: to foods such as nuts, gluten or shellfish, to insect stings, to medicines, or anything else. We pass food allergies on to every kitchen on the route. If you carry an adrenaline auto-injector or antihistamines, bring a spare and show your guide where you keep them.
I have a medical condition. Do I need to tell you?
Yes. Please tell us before you book about any medical condition, disability, pregnancy or mobility limitation, and any medicines you take regularly. We will ask you to fill in a short health form. This is not to turn people away: it lets us judge whether a trip suits you, plan the pace and know what to do in an emergency. Many of our routes are remote, and the nearest hospital can be several hours away by road.
Are the trips safe? What about snakes and large mammals?
Our guides keep a safe distance from wildlife, follow park rules, and never handle, bait or crowd animals. Snakes are watched from a respectful distance and only with a guide. Large mammals such as elephants and gaur are given plenty of room, and safaris follow the park's rules on vehicles and routes.

Practicalities

What do I need to arrange myself?
Your travel to the trip's start point and home from its end point (flights, trains and visa included); your camera gear, binoculars and walking or trekking gear; chargers, power banks and a plug adapter; and any personal medication with copies of your prescriptions. Comprehensive travel medical insurance is essential.
What plugs and voltage does India use?
India runs on 230 V at 50 Hz. Sockets are Type D (three round pins in a triangle, the most common), Type M (a larger three-pin version for heavy appliances) and Type C (the two-pin Europlug, which fits most Type D sockets). Most phone, camera and laptop chargers accept 100–240 V, but check the label; single-voltage appliances from 110–120 V countries need a converter, not just an adapter. Bring a universal adapter and a power bank, as power can be intermittent at remote lodges.
Will I have mobile signal and internet? Can I use my laptop?
Expect to be partly offline. Most towns on our routes have mobile data, but signal is weak or absent inside many forests and sanctuaries and on stretches of hill road, and lodge Wi-Fi, where there is any, can be slow. You are welcome to bring a laptop for the evenings, but please do not plan video calls or work deadlines during the trip. Phones must be kept on silent during park safaris, and some tiger reserves do not allow phones in core safari zones at all; your guide will brief you. Overseas visitors should check international roaming with their provider before travelling, and leave a copy of the itinerary with family.
Do you supply toilet paper, soap, toothpaste and other essentials?
The lodges and hotels we use normally supply soap and toilet paper in rooms. Please bring your own toothbrush, toothpaste and other toiletries. On the road and in forest areas, public toilets often have neither toilet paper nor soap, and many Indian toilets have a hand-held water spray instead of paper, so carry a small kit: tissues or a travel roll, hand sanitiser and wet wipes.
What accommodation can I expect?
We use wildlife lodges, guesthouses and nature stays chosen for comfort and for being close to the field. Each trip page shows the type of accommodation on that route.

Booking

How do I book a trip?
Message us on WhatsApp (+91 94431 61606) or send the enquiry form with the trip and dates you are interested in, your group size and any health or dietary needs. We will reply with availability, a suggested plan and how to confirm your place.
Are the listed dates fixed? How far ahead should I book?
The dates on each trip page are planned departures for the coming seasons, set by each trip's season and departure pattern. We list departures at least 6 weeks ahead, to leave time for park permits, lodges and your own travel plans. We confirm each date with you when you enquire. If none of them suits you, ask about a private departure. Parks occasionally close or suspend safaris at short notice for weather, wildlife-management or legal reasons. If that happens on your dates, we substitute a comparable site or activity and tell you as early as we can.

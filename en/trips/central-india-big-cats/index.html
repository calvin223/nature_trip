What we look for

The habitats and species this route is planned around.

Highlights

  • Jeep safaris in prime tiger and leopard habitat
  • Birding and nature walks in lodge grounds and buffer forests
  • Evening birding in lodge grounds outside the reserve for owls, nightjars and forest sounds (night tourism inside tiger reserves is not permitted)
  • Comfortable lodges, local food, and relaxed downtime between drives
  • Ethical viewing with guides who understand animal behaviour and park rules

Species we search for

  • Tigers
  • Leopards
  • Wild dogs
  • Owls
  • Raptors

What you take home

  • Enjoy ethical big-cat viewing across varied forest habitats
  • Understand animal tracks, alarm calls, and safari fieldcraft
  • Leave with a personal wildlife checklist and route notes
  • Balance safari intensity with rest, food, and comfortable travel
  • See India beyond a single habitat or species

What you might see

Wildlife sightings depend on the season, the weather and local conditions on the day of your visit. We plan routes and timings to give you the best chance, but no particular sighting can be guaranteed.

Is this trip right for you?

Easy

Mostly vehicle-based safaris, walks in lodge grounds and gentle trails. Suits most fitness levels, including families.

Pace
Easy. Most wildlife watching is from a safari jeep, walks stay in lodge grounds and buffer forest, and there is downtime between drives. Day 4 is a lighter day.
Terrain
Dry forest, meadows, waterholes and rocky forest edges, seen mostly from the vehicle.
Days & weather
Safari days have a morning and an afternoon drive. Early-morning drives can be cool, so bring a light jacket.

Who will love it

  • International visitors
  • Families
  • First-time safari guests
  • Photographers
  • Wildlife enthusiasts

Who it may not suit

Worth weighing up before you book:

  • Big-cat watching takes patience: long, quiet spells in the jeep, with no promise that a tiger will appear.
  • Heat late in the season: daytime highs in central India can pass 40 °C by the end of May.
  • No night drives: night tourism is not permitted inside tiger reserves, so after-dark birding is in lodge grounds outside the reserve.

The itinerary

Day 1 is the departure date.

Photographed at Kanha Tiger Reserve, Madhya Pradesh.

Photo: Kandukuru Nagarjun, CC BY 2.0

  1. Day 1 Arrival in Tiger Country

    Arrive, settle into your lodge, meet your naturalist, and take a short birding walk near the property.

  2. Day 2 First Safari Drives

    Morning and afternoon jeep safaris focused on tracks, calls, habitat signs, and patient big-cat viewing.

  3. Day 3 Birds, Meadows, and Waterholes

    Explore different safari zones with time for raptors, woodland birds, deer, wild boar, and possible predator movement near water.

  4. Day 4 Slow Wildlife Day

    A lighter day with optional village, river, or lodge-ground birding between safari sessions.

  5. Day 5 Leopards and Forest Edges

    Search rocky edges, dry forest, and grassland mosaics for leopards, sloth bears, owls, and smaller mammals.

  6. Day 6 Final Full Safari Day

    Use the final full day to revisit the most productive habitats and refine the group checklist.

  7. Day 7 Breakfast Review and Departure

    Review sightings, photographs, and route notes before departure after breakfast.

You arrange

Getting to the start and home again
You arrange and pay for your own travel to the trip's start point and home from its end point, including international and domestic flights, trains and any hotel nights before or after the trip. The start and end points are under "At a glance" on each trip page; where they say "Confirmed at booking", we give you the details when you book.
Passport and visa
Make sure your passport is valid and that you hold the right Indian visa before you travel. Apply only through the Government of India's official site, indianvisaonline.gov.in: look-alike sites charge several times the real fee.
Your own kit
Cameras, binoculars, walking shoes, rain gear, chargers and a plug adapter are yours to bring; we carry shared field guides and references. The packing list on Practical Info covers the rest.
Personal medication
Bring enough of any medication you take for the whole trip plus a few days' spare, in its original packaging with a copy of the prescription, and keep it in your hand luggage. Our guides carry a basic first-aid kit but cannot supply prescription medicines.

What to bring

  • Neutral-coloured comfortable clothing
  • Light jacket for early morning drives
  • Hat, sunglasses, and sunscreen
  • Personal medications
  • Binoculars if you have them
  • Camera or phone for photography

Why these months

Tiger-reserve core zones close for the monsoon from 1 July to 30 September, and some reserves close from mid-June. We stop at the end of May, when daytime highs in central India can pass 40 °C.

What we look for

The habitats and species this route is planned around.

Highlights

  • Dawn birding for Himalayan specialities in oak, pine, and rhododendron forests
  • Scenic village trails, viewpoints, and mountain photography stops
  • Flexible walking pace with warm meals and rest breaks built in
  • Search for mammals, raptors, woodpeckers, and mixed feeding flocks
  • Longer or higher-altitude extensions can be discussed on request

Species we search for

  • Pheasants
  • Laughingthrushes
  • Woodpeckers
  • Deer
  • Martens

What you take home

  • Enjoy Himalayan birds and mammals at a comfortable travel pace
  • Recognise key mountain habitats and seasonal wildlife patterns
  • Improve birding and wildlife observation in forested terrain
  • Experience local culture and scenery alongside wildlife
  • Take home a personal checklist and practical viewing tips

Wildlife sightings depend on the season, the weather and local conditions on the day of your visit. We plan routes and timings to give you the best chance, but no particular sighting can be guaranteed.

Is this trip right for you?

Moderate

More walking on uneven forest trails, some climbs, early starts and more exposure to rain, heat or cold. Needs basic fitness and comfort outdoors.

Pace
Moderate, at a flexible walking pace with warm meals and rest breaks built in, and a slower afternoon on Day 4.
Terrain
Forest trails through oak, pine and rhododendron, a ridge route with viewpoints, and village paths.
Days & weather
Dawn starts for birding, and cold mornings in the mountains: bring warm layers.

Who will love it

  • Birders
  • Nature lovers
  • Couples and friends
  • Photographers
  • Slow travellers

Who it may not suit

Worth weighing up before you book:

  • Higher ground: Day 5 visits a higher forest belt. If you have a heart or breathing condition, tell us before you book.
  • A plan that bends to the mountains: the high forest day runs only if conditions allow.

The itinerary

Day 1 is the departure date.

  1. 1

    Day 1: Arrival in the Foothills

    Arrive, settle in, and enjoy a short acclimatisation walk for common mountain birds.

  2. 2

    Day 2: Oak Forest Birding

    Early walk through oak and mixed forest, with time for woodpeckers, laughingthrushes, flycatchers, and raptors.

  3. 3

    Day 3: Ridge Trails and Viewpoints

    Follow a scenic ridge route with stops for birds, landscapes, tea, and local village life.

  4. 4

    Day 4: Pheasant and Mammal Search

    Look for shy forest birds and mammals during the most active hours, with a slower afternoon for rest or photography.

  5. 5

    Day 5: High Forest Day

    Visit a higher forest belt if conditions allow, focusing on mixed flocks, raptors, and mountain plant life.

  6. 6

    Day 6: Final Walk and Departure

    Short morning walk, checklist review, and departure after breakfast.

You arrange

Getting to the start and home again
You arrange and pay for your own travel to the trip's start point and home from its end point, including international and domestic flights, trains and any hotel nights before or after the trip. The start and end points are under "At a glance" on each trip page; where they say "Confirmed at booking", we give you the details when you book.
Passport and visa
Make sure your passport is valid and that you hold the right Indian visa before you travel. Apply only through the Government of India's official site, indianvisaonline.gov.in: look-alike sites charge several times the real fee.
Your own kit
Cameras, binoculars, walking shoes, rain gear, chargers and a plug adapter are yours to bring; we carry shared field guides and references. The packing list on Practical Info covers the rest.
Personal medication
Bring enough of any medication you take for the whole trip plus a few days' spare, in its original packaging with a copy of the prescription, and keep it in your hand luggage. Our guides carry a basic first-aid kit but cannot supply prescription medicines.

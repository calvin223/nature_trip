What we look for

The habitats and species this route is planned around.

Highlights

  • Guided night walks on private estate and homestay land outside protected areas, for frogs, geckos, snakes, owls, moths and rainforest soundscapes
  • Stream and forest-edge visits during peak amphibian activity
  • Waterfall, viewpoint, spice, and plantation stops between wildlife sessions
  • Safe snake observation from respectful distances
  • Monsoon photography support for low light, rain, and macro subjects

Species we search for

  • Frogs
  • Geckos
  • Pit vipers
  • Hornbills
  • Forest butterflies

What you take home

  • Experience tropical rainforest at its most alive
  • Observe frogs, reptiles, birds, and insects safely with a specialist guide
  • Build a personal list of monsoon species and photo subjects
  • Understand the rainforest by walking it, listening to it, and watching it after dark
  • Balance adventurous night walks with enough rest between sessions

What you might see

Wildlife sightings depend on the season, the weather and local conditions on the day of your visit. We plan routes and timings to give you the best chance, but no particular sighting can be guaranteed.

Is this trip right for you?

Moderate

More walking on uneven forest trails, some climbs, early starts and more exposure to rain, heat or cold. Needs basic fitness and comfort outdoors.

Pace
Moderate, with the main sessions after dark: guided night walks for frogs, geckos, snakes, owls and moths. Day 2 starts with a slower morning.
Terrain
Forest edges and stream banks in monsoon rain, often walked after dark by headlamp. Sturdy waterproof boots matter here.
Days & weather
Rain is part of the trip: the monsoon is when amphibians are most active. Bring reliable rain gear and a dry bag for electronics.

Who will love it

  • Wildlife enthusiasts
  • Adventure travellers
  • Photographers
  • Advanced naturalists
  • Small private groups

Who it may not suit

Worth weighing up before you book:

  • Leeches are common on wet forest paths in the monsoon. Long trousers and sturdy boots help.
  • Snakes are among the animals we look for. We watch them only from a safe distance with a guide, but if snakes worry you, this trip may not be for you.
  • Late evenings on uneven, wet ground in the dark, which needs steady footing.

The itinerary

Day 1 is the departure date.

Photographed at the Cardamom Hills, Kerala.

Photo: Ziegler175, CC BY-SA 3.0

  1. Day 1 Arrival and Rainforest Welcome

    Arrive, settle in, meet your guide, and take a short evening walk if weather allows.

  2. Day 2 Rainforest Streams After Dark

    A slower morning, forest-edge exploration after lunch, and a guided night walk along frog-rich streams on private land outside the protected area.

  3. Day 3 Reptiles, Waterfalls, and Viewpoints

    Daytime search for lizards and forest birds, followed by a waterfall or viewpoint visit and another optional night session.

  4. Day 4 Endemic Wildlife Focus

    Look for locally special frogs, birds, butterflies, and reptiles, with a relaxed evening checklist session.

  5. Day 5 Breakfast Review and Departure

    Review sightings and photo highlights over breakfast before departure after lunch.

You arrange

Getting to the start and home again
You arrange and pay for your own travel to the trip's start point and home from its end point, including international and domestic flights, trains and any hotel nights before or after the trip. The start and end points are under "At a glance" on each trip page; where they say "Confirmed at booking", we give you the details when you book.
Passport and visa
Make sure your passport is valid and that you hold the right Indian visa before you travel. Apply only through the Government of India's official site, indianvisaonline.gov.in: look-alike sites charge several times the real fee.
Your own kit
Cameras, binoculars, walking shoes, rain gear, chargers and a plug adapter are yours to bring; we carry shared field guides and references. The packing list on Practical Info covers the rest.
Personal medication
Bring enough of any medication you take for the whole trip plus a few days' spare, in its original packaging with a copy of the prescription, and keep it in your hand luggage. Our guides carry a basic first-aid kit but cannot supply prescription medicines.

What to bring
